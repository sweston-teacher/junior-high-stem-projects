### What Is STEM? What Is STEAM?
**Description:** Clear, student-friendly definitions and examples
#### Content:
- STEM = Science, Technology, Engineering, Math. STEAM adds Art for creativity and communication.
- Examples (big ideas):
  - <span class="tag stem-sci">Science</span>: Investigate questions about the natural world
  - <span class="tag stem-tech">Technology</span>: Tools and systems we use daily (apps, devices)
  - <span class="tag stem-eng">Engineering</span>: Design solutions to real problems
  - <span class="tag stem-math">Math</span>: Patterns, measurement, and data
  - <span class="tag stem-art">Art</span> (STEAM): Design/visuals/storytelling to explain ideas
- Class connection: We coded (micro:bit), built/tested (Ozobot tracks), and published (clock website)
---

### Engineering Design Process (EDP)
**Description:** The cycle engineers use to solve real problems
#### Content:
- Typical steps (loop, not a straight line):
  - <span class="chip chip-ask">Ask</span>: What is the problem? Who has it? Why does it matter?
  - <span class="chip chip-imagine">Imagine</span>: Brainstorm many ideas (no judging yet)
  - <span class="chip chip-plan">Plan</span>: Choose a promising idea and make a plan
  - <span class="chip chip-create">Create</span>: Build a first version (prototype)
  - <span class="chip chip-test">Test</span>: Try it out, collect data
  - <span class="chip chip-improve">Improve</span>: Use what you learned to make it better, repeat
- Classroom mindset: Iteration is normal; “First draft” is supposed to be rough
- We will use EDP thinking throughout the careers project: Ask → Plan → Create → Share → Improve

### Research Checklist (What To Find)
**Description:** Clear questions students must answer
#### Content:
- What the job is: 1–2 sentences in your own words
- Who it helps or what problem it solves
- Typical tasks/responsibilities (3–5 bullets)
- Education/training path (HS → college/certs → on-the-job)
- Tools/software commonly used
- Salary range (entry–experienced; source it)
- Job outlook (growing? stable?) + at least one local example employer
- One curiosity hook: surprising fact or inspiring story

### What Is Citing a Source?
**Description:** How to show where you got information
#### Content:
- When you use a fact or picture, tell where it came from.
- On the slide, add a tiny line: <em>Source: NASA</em> or <em>Source: Code.org</em>
- At the end, make a Works Cited slide with the links.
- Example (on a facts slide):
  - “Engineers use the design process to improve ideas.” <small>(Source: NASA)</small>
  - Works Cited slide: “NASA — Engineering Design Process: https://example-link”
- Use good sources: .edu, .gov, big organizations, or company pages
- If AI helped you think, still check a real source and cite that
---

### Sources and Academic Honesty
**Description:** How to research safely and cite properly
#### Content:
- Put information in your own words; don’t copy/paste paragraphs
- If you quote, use quotation marks and say where it’s from
- Works Cited slide: clear URLs are fine (MLA/APA not required)
