STEM Careers Research Project — Student One-Pager

Teams research a real STEM/STEAM career, build a polished Canva presentation, and teach the class what they discover.
Mission Team up to explore a real STEM or STEAM career, collect reliable facts, and present what you learn.
Timeline 1-week project (5 class periods)
Due Date Insert date here
Canvas Submission Share link to your Canva presentation (view access) with a Works Cited slide included.

Team Setup & Daily Targets

  • Teams of 2–3 with different careers (get approval for new ideas).
  • Create a shared research doc on Day 1 with both names at the top.
  • Stay on pace with the daily goals below:
Day Focus Must have by the end
1 Launch & career choice Career selected and shared research doc started
2 Canva login & template Canva account ready, template chosen, Title slide made
3 Research & slide planning 3–4 trustworthy facts gathered, slide titles drafted
4 Build slides & peer check 2–3 content slides built, teammates review against checklist
5 Presentations Slides complete, both partners speak 2–4 minutes, ready for one question

Research Checklist (complete all)

  • What the job is (1–2 sentences in your own words).
  • Who it helps or what problem it solves.
  • Typical tasks or responsibilities (3–5 bullet points).
  • Education and training path (high school → college/certs → on-the-job).
  • Tools or software commonly used.
  • Salary range (entry through experienced) with a cited source.
  • Job outlook or demand plus one local or regional employer example.
  • One curiosity hook: surprising fact, story, or innovation related to the career.
  • At least three trustworthy sources saved for Works Cited (no AI-only answers).

Slide Plan (5–8 slides recommended)

  1. Title: Career name, team names, class/period.
  2. Career Snapshot: What the job is and who it helps.
  3. Path to Get There: Education, training, certifications.
  4. Tools & Daily Work: Key responsibilities, technology, or equipment.
  5. Salary & Outlook: Pay range, demand, local employer example.
  6. Inspiring Highlight: Story, project, or impact that makes the career exciting.
  7. Works Cited: Minimum of three sources with clear URLs or titles (last slide).
Need more space? Add extra slides for visuals or quotes, but keep text large and concise.

Canva Quick Start

  1. Go to canva.com → Log in → choose Continue with Google using your school email.
  2. Choose Presentation (16:9) and pick a simple, easy-to-read template.
  3. Rename the file: Period-Last1&Last2-Career (example: 3-Weston&Miller-DataScientist).
  4. Add slide titles first, then fill in details once your research is ready.
  5. Use high-contrast colors, big fonts, and relevant visuals (credit the artist/source).
  6. Copy the Share link and set it to “Anyone with the link can view” before submitting.

Source Expectations & Academic Honesty

  • Put information in your own words; use quotation marks for any direct quotes.
  • Cite the source on the slide (small text is fine) and again on the Works Cited slide.
  • Reliable sources: .edu, .gov, professional organizations, reputable companies.
  • If AI helped you brainstorm, confirm facts with real sources you can cite.

Presentation Day Expectations

  • Both teammates speak during the 2–4 minute presentation.
  • Face the audience, project your voice, and refer to the slides—not just reading them.
  • Slides should be clean: large text, few bullet points, purposeful visuals.
  • Be ready to answer one audience question using evidence from your research.

Quick Rubric (4 points each)

Criteria Highly Proficient (4) Proficient (3)
Research & Accuracy Every checklist item covered with specific, verified details and examples. All checklist items addressed with accurate facts.
Slide Design & Organization Slides are visually clear, balanced, and easy to follow with a smooth flow. Slides are readable and mostly organized with only minor issues.
Speaking & Collaboration Confident delivery, equal participation, eye contact, handles Q&A with evidence. Clear delivery with both partners contributing; answers question with basic evidence.
Sources & Citations Three or more strong sources cited on slides and Works Cited; all paraphrased or properly quoted. Required sources listed; citations mostly present and correct.
